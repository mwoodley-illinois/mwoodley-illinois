# Michael Woodley

I'm an Assistant Clinical Professor of Computer Science at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where I teach software engineering through large, project-based courses that emphasize teamwork, professional practice, and building real software.

Before joining academia, I spent more than twenty years in the software industry leading development teams and building commercial software. My work included Microsoft Flight Simulator at subLOGIC, combat flight simulation at Mission Studios, CAD systems, reverse engineering proprietary file formats, and a variety of engineering tools. Those experiences continue to shape how I think about software engineering and how I teach it.

My current interests focus on preparing students for modern software engineering practice. As AI becomes a standard part of professional development, I'm exploring how engineering education should evolve while preserving the professional judgment, verification skills, accountability, and trustworthiness that distinguish effective engineers. Much of my recent work centers on designing instructional workflows that encourage thoughtful use of AI rather than treating it as a replacement for engineering thinking.

I enjoy designing systems as much as I enjoy building software. Whether improving the operation of a large software engineering course, developing new instructional approaches, or solving an engineering problem, I'm usually interested in understanding the system before optimizing the parts.

Outside of computing, I enjoy woodworking, restoring and tuning quality tools, music, and finding well-engineered solutions—whether they're software, hand planes, musical instruments, or audio equipment.
Outside of teaching and software, I’m an avid woodworker and a lifelong music lover, always in search of a good design challenge or a better sound system.
